What Falconhead homes are like

Falconhead grew up around Falconhead Golf Club, a public fee course, and the first homes went in during the early 2000s. There are five sections and roughly a thousand homes. Sizes run wide, from 1,300 square feet up to about 6,200, and lots go from a quarter acre to a full acre. Plenty of them back onto a fairway or the greenbelt. Part of the community is gated and part is open, so the first question on any job is whether we need a code. Most houses wear stucco, Hill Country limestone, or both on the same wall.

That spread of ages shows up in the glass. A two decade old window has seen twenty summers of sprinklers and sun, while the newer builds carry bigger glass runs and taller entries. Everyone answers to the Falconhead HOA, so vendor rules feel familiar from one street to the next.

What Falconhead windows deal with

Golf course irrigation is the big one. Fairway sprinklers throw hard water onto back walls and patio doors, and when it dries it leaves white mineral spots. Left alone for a few seasons those spots can etch into the glass and stop coming off with a normal wash. Add cedar pollen from December to February and live oak pollen in March and April, and a back wall that looked fine in the morning is hazy by lunch. Our dual RO and DI system rinses with water near 0 TDS, so nothing dries on the pane.

Stucco and limestone need their own care. We never pressure wash limestone. The 12V soft wash system lets us lift the green film and dust off those walls without driving water into the stone or the joints. Window tracks on the golf side collect grass clippings and webs, and we clean them for $5 a track.

How we work Falconhead

Because the community is only partly gated, we ask for the gate code when you text for a quote and keep it on the job sheet. We tend to book several Falconhead sections on the same day, along with Falconhead West and the Spillman streets, so you often get a slot that fits a route we already run. Many owners here pair exterior windows with a house soft wash, since the same visit covers the stucco, limestone and glass. Water fed poles reach up to three stories from the ground, which suits the taller course side elevations without ladders on the walls.

For a 3,500 square foot home, exterior windows come to about $350, and on site that is around two and a half to three and a half hours. Adding the inside brings it to about $525 at $15 a window and roughly another hour and three quarters.

Falconhead questions

Can you get the sprinkler spots off my back windows?

Usually, yes. Fresh mineral spots from golf course overspray come off with our hard water removal, which is $10 per pane. Spots that have sat for years and etched the glass may only improve, and we tell you that before we start.

Do you need a gate code for Falconhead?

Parts of Falconhead are gated and parts are not. Text us your address when you ask for a quote and send the code if there is one. We note it on the job sheet and do not ask again.

Is it safe to clean my limestone walls?

Yes, with a soft wash. We never use a pressure washer on limestone because it pits the stone and forces water into the joints. The 12V soft wash system cleans it gently.

How much is a typical Falconhead home?

Exterior windows are $10 each or about $0.10 per square foot of home, with a $200 minimum. A 3,500 square foot house is about $350, and inside and out is $15 a window.

What if spots show up after you leave?

Text us a photo within 7 days and we come back free when we are in the area. Every job also carries our 100% satisfaction guarantee.

How often should golf side glass be cleaned?

Many fairway lots do well on a triannual or quarterly plan. Those plans save 15% and include hard water removal on up to 5 windows each visit, which helps with overspray.
