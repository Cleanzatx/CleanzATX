What Bee Creek Estates homes are like

With only 38 homes, Bee Creek Estates feels private. It is a gated acreage enclave with a Bee Creek Estates HOA, and homes are priced from about $1.2M to $2.5M. Lots roll over the Hill Country, so houses sit well apart and many have long driveways and hill views. Because the neighborhood is small and each home is custom or semi custom, the architecture varies, with limestone, stucco and large timber trim all common. The Spicewood mailing address and the Bee Cave edge put it near shopping without any of the traffic.

Windows tend to be large and numerous, since owners buy here for the view.

What Bee Creek Estates windows deal with

Acreage means native cedar and live oak right up to the house. Cedar pollen blankets glass from December to February, and live oak pollen follows in spring. Spiders love eaves that sit under trees, so webs gather around frames and tracks. Rural roads and gravel drives lift limestone dust that settles on lower windows, and wells or irrigation lines with hard water leave white mineral spots where sprinklers reach the wall. Sunset facing glass over the hills shows every streak.

Wind across open ground keeps the dust coming, so even a recently cleaned window can look dull after a dry week.

How we work Bee Creek Estates

The gate comes first. Send us the code or the person to call, and we confirm timing by text on the day. Long driveways mean we bring everything in one trip: a skid with 200 gallons of water on board, so we do not depend on your outdoor spigot. Water fed poles reach up to three stories from the ground, which keeps ladders off limestone. Indoors at Bee Creek Estates, panes are hand squeegeed over drop cloths. Houses on acreage often add a house soft wash, a roof soft wash for lichen and algae, and gutters, since cedar needles clog them.
