What Westcliff homes are like

Westcliff sits near the corner where RM 2222 meets Loop 360, a spot that feels more remote than the map suggests. Lots run from about 1.3 to 4 acres, and homes were built between 1987 and 2003. Prices land around $800K to $2M, so the section has both original custom houses and ones that have been updated. It is a small neighborhood with no HOA listed, so each owner keeps up their own place. You see long gravel or paved drives, native live oak and cedar, and yards that blend into the hills. Many houses back onto a slope or a wooded gully.

The upside is space and privacy. The downside, for glass, is that nature is always close.

What Westcliff windows deal with

Homes from the late 1980s through the early 2000s often have older frames and screens that have taken a lot of sun. Hard water from sprinklers has had years to bake into the lower panes. Where spotting has set, hard water spot removal is $10 per pane, and pure water cleaning afterward keeps new spots from coming back. Our dual RO, 4 stage system with a DI resin tank gives water near 0 TDS, so cleaned glass dries without a film. Water fed poles reach up to three stories, and tall gable windows on these older plans are common.

Tree cover means spider webs on eaves and leaf litter in tracks. Cedar pollen hits December to February, followed by live oak pollen in March and April. Screens are $5, tracks are $5, and solar screens are $15.
