What Homestead homes are like

The Homestead is built around views. Homes sit on lots of an acre or more, many on hilltops, and the newer Mediterranean and Hill Country designs lean into that with big glass facing the valley. Sizes run from 3,500 to 6,500 square feet, and prices start around $1M and go past $2M. There is a Homestead HOA and a MUD, but no gate at the entrance. Roofs are often tile or standing seam, walls are stucco or limestone, and the entries tend to be grand, with tall doors and clerestory windows high above the foyer.

That much glass is the point of living up here, so it is worth keeping clear.

What Homestead windows deal with

Hilltop houses get wind, and wind carries dust. Open lots around The Homestead mean fine limestone dust and construction grit blow across the glass all year, and nearby building does not help. Cedar pollen coats everything from December through February, and live oak pollen follows in March and April. Sprinklers watering a large lawn leave hard water spots on lower panes and sometimes overspray the upper ones too. West facing glass looking at the sunset shows every streak after a rushed cleaning, which is why pure water, with water near 0 TDS and no soap film, makes such a visible difference here.

Screens on big sliders collect webs and leaf litter fast, so we offer to clean them and the tracks at the same visit.

How we work The Homestead

Access is simple: no gate, so we drive up and set up. Water fed poles reach up to three stories from the ground, which handles most Homestead exteriors without a single ladder against stucco or stone. For tall foyer windows inside, we use cotton covered ladders and drop cloths. Because these houses are big, we often book a half day and pair windows with a soft wash on stucco or limestone, which we never pressure wash. A skid with 200 gallons on board means we are not tied to your spigot. Homes this size also pair well with a gutter cleaning, and we price flatwork separately at $0.18 per square foot for concrete.

A 4,500 square foot home comes to about $450 for the exterior glass. Plan on roughly three and a half to four and a half hours outside, plus about two hours inside.

The Homestead questions

How much does it cost to clean a large Homestead home?

Exterior windows are about $0.10 per square foot of home, so a 5,000 square foot house is about $500. Cleaning inside and out in The Homestead is $15 per window. We confirm the count by text before the job.

Do you need to come inside?

Only for interior glass. Exterior windows are cleaned from the ground with water fed poles. If you want inside too, we use drop cloths and hand squeegees, and ladders with cotton covers for tall rooms.

Can you reach second and third story windows on a hilltop house?

Yes, up to three stories from the ground with the water fed poles. On sloped lots we look at the ground first, and we tell you before the job if any glass needs a different approach.

Is there anything special about stucco or limestone here?

Yes. We never pressure wash limestone. We use a 12V soft wash system at low pressure, which cleans stucco and limestone without damaging the surface.

Do you offer a recurring plan?

Yes, the Clean Windows Club. Twice a Year is 10% off, Triannual and Quarterly 15%, and Every 2 Months 20%. The higher tiers include a free yearly screen cleaning and hard water removal on up to 5 windows each visit.
